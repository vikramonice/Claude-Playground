RESEARCH

RESEARCH INTERESTS

Antarctic Glaciology, Geophysical data collection and analysis (including ground-penetrating radar and GNSS systems), Ice-flow modelling, Geographical Information Systems (GIS).

RESEARCH FOCUS

Ice Shelf and Ice Rise Dynamics

Understanding the stability and evolution of Antarctic ice shelves and ice rises, with particular focus on Dronning Maud Land in East Antarctica. Using combined field observations and numerical modelling to investigate how these systems respond to changes in ocean and atmospheric forcing.

Geophysical Methods

Development and application of geophysical field techniques including:

  • Ground-penetrating radar (GPR) for ice thickness and internal stratigraphy
  • Active seismic surveys for sub-ice-shelf cavity exploration
  • GNSS positioning and surface velocity measurements
  • Autonomous phase-sensitive radar (ApRES) for ice deformation studies

Paleoclimate Reconstruction

Using internal ice stratigraphy from radar data and ice cores to reconstruct past changes in surface mass balance and climate conditions over millennial timescales. This work helps constrain ice sheet models and understand natural climate variability in Antarctica.

Ice-Ocean Interactions

Investigating the interaction between floating ice shelves and the ocean beneath them, particularly focusing on basal melting processes and their impact on ice shelf stability. Recent work includes the first dedicated seismic exploration beneath Nivlisen Ice Shelf (SENS project).

CURRENT RESEARCH

My current research at NCPOR focuses on:

  • Analysis of geophysical data from the SENS expedition (Nivlisen Ice Shelf seismic and radar surveys)
  • Processing ice core and radar data from Canisteo Peninsula (RAICA-CP expedition)
  • Regional characterization of ice rises in Dronning Maud Land
  • Development of ice-flow models to simulate observed internal stratigraphy
  • Synthesis of field and satellite data for mass balance studies

I am a glaciologist at the National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research (NCPOR) in Goa, India, where I lead the Glaciology & Geophysics group within the Antarctic Cryospheric Studies programme.

My research focuses on understanding the stability and evolution of Antarctic ice shelves and ice rises using geophysical field methods — primarily ground-penetrating radar, active seismic surveys, GNSS, and autonomous phase-sensitive radar (ApRES) — combined with ice-flow modelling. I'm particularly interested in how coastal ice masses in East Antarctica are responding to changes in ocean and atmospheric forcing, and what their internal structure can tell us about past climate variability.

Much of my work is built around collaborative, multinational field expeditions. I have led or co-led geophysical survey campaigns across Dronning Maud Land and West Antarctica, working with teams from Norway, the US, South Korea, and the UK. Most recently, I led the SENS project — the first dedicated sub-ice-shelf seismic exploration of Nivlisen Ice Shelf — and co-led the geophysical component of the RAICA-CP expedition to Canisteo Peninsula in West Antarctica.

I hold a Ph.D. in Antarctic Glaciology from the University of Oslo (supervised by Kenichi Matsuoka, Jon Ove Hagen, and Thamban Meloth), an M.Tech from IIT Kharagpur, and a B.Tech in Aerospace Engineering. Before joining NCPOR, I was a research fellow at the Norwegian Polar Institute in Tromsø.

Beyond research, I serve as India's National Representative to the SCAR GeoSciences Group and am actively involved in the SCAR RINGS Action Group. I'm committed to mentoring early-career polar researchers and have been part of the APECS leadership for several years.

RESEARCH INTERESTS

Antarctic Glaciology, Geophysical data collection and analysis (including ground-penetrating radar and GNSS systems), Ice-flow modelling, Geographical Information Systems (GIS).
